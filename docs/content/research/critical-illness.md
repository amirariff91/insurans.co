## 1. The condition list — 36 critical illnesses (verified from primary sources)

**Count: 36** is the industry-standard severe-stage set. All Malaysian insurers were required by **Bank Negara Malaysia to adopt standardized CI definitions for new and renewed policies on or after 1 April 2016** (MSIG announcement, read 2026-09-12: msig.com.my/highlights/standardization-of-critical-illness-definitions/). A "45-condition industry list" was **not found** on any PIAM/LIAM/BNM/insurer page; larger counts (100–180) exist only as per-product marketing expansions.

Canonical list, verified verbatim from AIA's official "36 Critical Illnesses Listing" PDF (read 2026-09-12: aia.com.my/content/dam/my/en/docs/our-products/36_Critical_Illnesses_Listing.pdf); BM renderings shown where standard, otherwise standard BM usage marked:

So "36" is the standard; the exact names can differ per insurer/certificate.

## 2. Short explainers (cited)

**Early vs late stage.** Standard 36 definitions are severe-stage — e.g. cancer "of specified severity" explicitly excluding "very early cancer" (HLB takaful appendix). Early-stage benefits pay a **partial, accelerated amount on earlier diagnosis**, reducing the sum assured while keeping the balance for a later end-stage claim. Verified worked example (Zurich Early Care Critical Illness page, read 2026-09-12): carcinoma-in-situ pays **50% of rider sum assured** (+12% rehabilitation monthly ×12 months + RM2,000 second medical opinion); end-stage kidney failure later pays **100% of the remaining 50%**; early stage covered to age 85, end stage to age 100. [INFERENCE from one verified product] Industry-wide, early payouts are commonly percentage-based while end-stage pays 100%.

**Rider vs standalone.** A **rider** attaches to a base policy: Prudential's Crisis Cover Benefit is officially an "insurance rider… add-on to a basic plan", attachable to PRUCash, paying an "advanced payout from your basic plan's coverage" on any of 36 CIs (prudential.com.my/en/products/riders/crisis-cover-benefit/, read 2026-09-12) — i.e. accelerated against the base sum assured. Great Eastern's SMART Early Payout CriticalCare is a "unit-deduction rider attachable to selected regular premium investment-linked insurance plans", must attach with the IL Critical Illness Benefit Rider, and benefits "accelerate" both that rider and the base IL plan (greateasternlife.com product page, read 2026-09-12). A **standalone** plan is bought on its own: Etiqa Critical Care Plus Insurance is sold directly online via EPF i-Lindung with no base plan (etiqa.com.my page, read 2026-09-12).

**SOCSO/PERKESO & mySalam vs private CI.** PERKESO's Invalidity Scheme is **occupation-based, not a disease-list product**: a permanent morbid condition leaving the insured incapable of earning at least 1/3 of customary earnings, certified by a Medical Board; benefits are a monthly invalidity pension/grant, constant-attendance allowance and dialysis at panel facilities — for employees with sufficient contribution records (perkeso.gov.my/en/our-services/protection/invalidity-scheme.html, read 2026-09-12). No lump-sum CI payout, no early-stage concept. **mySalam** (verified via MOF press release PDF, read 2026-09-12: mof.gov.my/portal/arkib/media/2019/feb/sm260219_Laman_Web_MySalam.pdf): free B40 health-protection scheme launched 24 Jan 2019, protection from 1 Jan 2019, covering **36 critical illnesses with RM8,000 one-off cash** plus **RM50/day hospital income (max 14 days/year = RM700/year)**, funded by a **RM2 billion** Great Eastern Holdings contribution; treatment at government hospitals. Contrast: private CI = own-premium, chosen sum assured, standardized definitions; mySalam = fixed RM8k for qualifying B40; SOCSO = pension based on incapacity.

**Takaful penyakit kritikal.** Payout purpose matches conventional CI (lump sum on diagnosis) but the akad differs: **tabarru'** (mutual donation fund), **wakalah** (agency), free of riba/gharar/maisir, with possible surplus/muhibah sharing; death benefits can pass as **hibah mutlak** to named beneficiaries under IFSA 2013 rather than through wasi/faraid administration (secondary explainers, read 2026-09-12: sunlifemalaysia.com/life-moments/bright-facts/takaful-vs-insurance/, ringgitplus.com/en/blog/the-experts-corner/the-difference-between-takaful-and-conventional-insurance.html, hibahtakafulmalaysia.com/2025/05/perbezaan-insurans-hibah-dan-takaful.html — flag: akad mechanics sourced from explainers, not BNM). Verified takaful product: **Etiqa Critical Care Takaful** under Etiqa Family Takaful Berhad, "licensed under Islamic Financial Services Act 2013", covering **39 critical illnesses**, RM50,000 sum covered from as little as RM30/year, no medical check-up, purchased via EPF i-Lindung (etiqa.com.my flyer PDF, read 2026-09-12).
